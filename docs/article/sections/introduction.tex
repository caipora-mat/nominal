\section{Nominal reasoning and selected actions}
\label{sec:introduction}

Reasoning about languages with binders requires a precise account of renaming
and dependence on names. Nominal sets express this dependence through finite
support: a finite set of atoms supports an object when every finite permutation
fixing those atoms pointwise also fixes the object. The definition applies to
syntax, functions and relations once their permutation actions have been
specified. We formalize the permutation, action and finite-support theory in
Lean, using the classical setting of nominal-set theory described by
Pitts~\cite[Chapters 1--2]{pitts2013}.

The choice of action is part of the mathematical interface. The same underlying
carrier can support a trivial action, an action inherited from its components,
or, for suitable carriers, a conjugation action. These choices yield different
invariance and support properties. Our representation makes the selected
action explicit through Mathlib's \texttt{MulAction} interface and keeps the
atom and carrier universes independent. Finite permutations form a subgroup of
\texttt{Equiv.Perm}; a distinct carrier represents data on which permutations
act trivially. These choices permit standard group-action reasoning while
preventing an implicit identification of mathematically different actions.

Two results connect this representation to support proofs. First, a finite
permutation factors into transpositions whose endpoints belong to its original
moved-point set. A permutation fixing a set pointwise therefore admits a
factorization that avoids that set. This yields a swap criterion for invariance
under any selected action, without an infinitude assumption. Second, over an
infinite atom type, finite supports are closed under binary intersection.
An inclusion-minimal supporting bound is consequently least among all finite
supporting bounds. This gives canonical support for an individually supported
element, without assuming that its entire carrier is nominal.
A two-atom counterexample shows why the latter conclusion cannot be inferred
from the former without the additional hypothesis.

Section~\ref{sec:foundations} develops controlled factorization and explains
the action choices that matter to the interface. Section~\ref{sec:finite-support}
uses these results to derive finite-support laws, including transport by
conjugation and intersection, and constructs least support in
Section~\ref{sec:least-support}. Section~\ref{sec:perspectives} relates the action
and support interfaces to predicate reasoning and other nominal formalizations.
The Lean interfaces discussed here are exported by
\texttt{import Package} in the namespace \texttt{NominalPackage}.
